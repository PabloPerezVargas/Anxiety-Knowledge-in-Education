# Instrument development

The project will develop three instruments, one per workshop: Anxiety first,
Decision-making second, and Risk later. They are separate instruments; items
and scores are not assumed equivalent across workshops.

## Current status and next decisions

Development is at feasibility scoping; the Anxiety blueprint and candidate
items remain to be drafted. No A/B forms or pilot data exist, and empirical
form equivalence has not been established. Planned content review is not
completed validation.

No fixed test length or item count has been decided. Agree administration
time, whether perceived-knowledge judgements should precede the response
options, and whether vignettes belong in the first version or a later one.
Historical response coding and additional independent reviewers are possible
later contributions, not work already done.

The workshop developer has confirmed that counterbalancing two paper forms
within a class is feasible in principle: pre A/post B for one group and pre
B/post A for another. This confirms the delivery arrangement only, not the
existence or equivalence of the forms.

## Blueprint and review

Start with workshop objectives, then develop candidate items and plausible
distractors, conduct structured content review, and pilot the items. Diagnose
weak historical performance before changing the blueprint: it may reflect
baseline knowledge, delivery, scoring or item design as well as content.
Historical open responses may inform plausible distractors, but misconception
coding has not been done. See the [workshop content framework](../docs/workshop_content_framework.md)
and [evidence-to-design synthesis](../docs/evidence_to_instrument_design.md)
for how the historical findings can inform Track B without dictating item retention.

Multiple choice is proposed to standardise scoring and make item-level
patterns easier to examine. It measures recognition rather than free recall
and remains sensitive to guessing, wording and distractor quality. Planned
structured content review will consider relevance, clarity and alignment; it
is a review process, not evidence that an instrument has been validated. The
workshop developer can contribute content expertise and the project owner
psychological and methodological expertise; additional independent reviewers
would strengthen the review if available.

## Perceived knowledge and application

The proposed paper format collects item-by-item Yes/No perceived-knowledge
judgements before participants see the response options. Its feasibility and
section order remain to be agreed and piloted. These judgements could support
item-level calibration; the historical digital data contain only an aggregate
0–8 count.

Vignettes may assess application in realistic situations. Examine application
and conceptual knowledge separately unless empirical evidence supports
combining them.

## Forms and later analyses

Develop matched A/B forms and compare item performance, distractor use, score
distributions and form effects using pilot data. Matching objectives does not
establish empirical equivalence. Examine dimensionality before selecting
further psychometric analyses. IRT, DIF, formal equating, anchors and a larger
item bank are later options only if the instruments, data and research
questions support them. A larger item bank remains a proposal awaiting
agreement, and none of these analyses is a prerequisite for the historical
work or the first blueprint.

Record item sets, forms and scoring rules as versions are produced. Historical
open-response and future multiple-choice scores remain distinct unless a study
supports linking them. Original instruments, scoring keys and participant
responses remain private. See [methodology](../docs/methodology.md),
[limitations](../docs/limitations_and_future_work.md) and [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md)
for the wider project.
