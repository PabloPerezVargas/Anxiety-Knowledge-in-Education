# Conceptual framework

## Four research targets

The programme distinguishes four targets: **metacognitive judgement** (“Do I
think I know this?”), **knowledge performance** (“Can I demonstrate that I
know it?”), **applied reasoning** (“Can I identify or use the relevant concept
in context?”), and **real-world behaviour** (“Does the person respond
differently in everyday life?”). They are related questions, not sequential
stages or a proven causal chain. **Retention**—whether an outcome persists over
time—is a separate dimension.

The historical archive mainly captures aggregate perceived knowledge and
immediate open-response performance intended to assess declarative or
conceptual knowledge. It does not establish later recognition, application,
behaviour, coping, clinical prevention, or retention.

## Recall and recognition

Historical open responses mainly require recall or production. Proposed
multiple-choice items would rely more on recognition. These are different ways
of observing knowledge, not successive stages; neither format is automatically
superior. Open responses may depend partly on verbal production and scoring,
while recognition formats introduce guessing, wording, reading-demand, and
distractor-quality considerations. Format should match the intended outcome
and preserve content coverage.

## Metacognitive calibration

The historical digital data contain perceived knowledge only as an aggregate
0–8 count. They cannot show whether a participant's judgement matched
performance on a particular item. In a future assessment, perceived knowledge
could be recorded before showing response options and compared with the item
result:

| Perceived judgement | Item result |
|---|---|
| Yes | Correct |
| Yes | Incorrect |
| No | Correct |
| No | Incorrect |

These four combinations could describe alignment or mismatch between perceived
and demonstrated knowledge. They are observations for metacognitive calibration,
not clinical categories or stable traits.

## Application

Vignette-based items could examine whether participants recognise or use a
concept in a realistic situation. Initially, applied reasoning should remain
separate from conceptual knowledge. A response to a vignette is still not
evidence of real-world behaviour.

## Current evidence boundary

The historical archive mainly supports conclusions about immediate assessed
knowledge. It does not establish retention, later recognition, contextual
application, real-world behaviour, improved coping, or clinical prevention.
The same PRE and POST content may also contribute familiarity or pretest
sensitisation to observed change; there was no untreated comparison.
