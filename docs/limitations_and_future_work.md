# Limitations and future work

These limitations guide interpretation and future work; proposed improvements
are not completed analyses or evidence of validity. The instrument's current
stage is described in [instrument development](../instrument/README.md).

## Historical workshop data

| Limitation | Interpretation and possible next step |
|---|---|
| The same questions were asked immediately before and after the workshop. | Familiarity, practice and pretest sensitisation may contribute to change. Report observed pre/post change, not an isolated causal workshop effect. Counterbalanced forms may reduce repeated-item effects; a no-pretest comparison could be considered later if feasible. |
| Assessment was immediate and measured test performance. | Retention and real-world or clinical behaviour are not established. Delayed follow-up or behavioural outcomes would require new data and a separate design. |
| Historical items were open-ended and scored against key concepts. | Language, expression and scoring decisions may affect results. Check the scoring rules before interpreting differences in observed score ranges; independent rescoring of a sample could estimate scorer agreement. |
| Workshops and school years are not fully crossed; participants are grouped within centres and classes. | Context and workshop differences are partly confounded, and uncertainty should account for clustering where identifiers allow. The 2025/26 cohort is the only one with all three workshops; their distinct instruments still do not make raw scores equivalent. |
| Anxiety historical scores vary by cohort and centre composition; scoring/version comparability is uncertain. | The 2021/22 Anxiety cohort comes from one centre; item score distributions and item relationships differ across years. No year-specific scoring or administration record establishes comparability. Interpret pooled Anxiety item patterns cautiously and confirm versions/scoring with the workshop developer. |
| POST dimensional analyses are exploratory and differ by workshop and Anxiety cohort inclusion. | Risk and recent-cohort Anxiety are more compatible with a broad POST dimension, while pooled Anxiety and Decision-making show mixed evidence. This does not validate unidimensionality; totals remain descriptive summaries and the factor pattern should not determine item retention. |
| Perceived knowledge is a 0–8 count, while objective knowledge is a sum of scored items. | These are different metrics. Aggregate associations can be examined, but the digital data do not support per-item calibration. If paper records contain item-level judgements, digitising them is an optional, high-effort step. |
| Historical and proposed response formats differ. | Open-response scores and future multiple-choice scores are not assumed interchangeable. A bridge study could examine format effects if linking them becomes important. |

## New instruments and forms

The proposed paper instruments have not been pilot-tested or validated.
Multiple choice measures recognition rather than free recall and can be
affected by guessing, wording, distractor quality and reading demands. Brief
items, structured content review and pilot analysis can address some of these
issues, but do not by themselves establish validity. Additional independent
reviewers would strengthen content review if available.

Counterbalanced forms may reduce repeated-item effects, but related pre-test
content can still sensitise participants, and form scores should not be treated
as interchangeable without empirical evidence. The [instrument development
guide](../instrument/README.md) describes the proposed forms and their current
status.

Vignettes may assess application; examine it separately from conceptual
knowledge unless evidence supports combining them. Further psychometric
analyses depend on appropriate instruments and adequate data.

## Optional historical work

These additions are not prerequisites for the planned historical analyses:

- Check whether paper questionnaires contain per-item perceived-knowledge
  responses; digitisation could enable item-level calibration.
- Code a manageable sample of open responses for misconceptions that may
  inform distractors. This has not been done.
- Independently rescore a sample of open responses if the original records
  and an additional evaluator are available.

Participant responses remain private. Any public data release requires a
separate privacy review, including small-group and centre-level risks.
