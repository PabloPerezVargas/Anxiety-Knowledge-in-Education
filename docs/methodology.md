# Methodology

The study connects analysis of the historical workshop data with development
of new assessment instruments. Historical analyses can proceed without waiting
for new items or forms.

## Historical design and measures

Participants completed the same eight-question assessment immediately before
and after a workshop. Historical questions were open-ended and scored against
key concepts. Anxiety, Decision-making and Risk each have a distinct
instrument: the same item number can address different content, so item
responses and totals are not presumed equivalent across workshops.

**Objective knowledge** is the sum of the eight scored items at each
measurement. **Perceived knowledge** is the count (0–8) of questions a
participant believed they could answer. It is recorded in the source columns
`conoc_tot_pre` and `conoc_tot_post`, despite their misleading names. These
measures use different metrics: examine their relationships using their
original values, not by subtracting or rescaling one against the other. The
digital historical data contain perceived totals, not per-item judgements, so
per-item overconfidence and underestimation cannot be calculated.

Workshop and school year are not fully crossed: 2021/22 contains Anxiety only,
2024/25 contains Anxiety and Decision-making, and 2025/26 contains all three.
If making a between-workshop contrast, 2025/26 is the only common cohort; this
does not make the distinct instruments equivalent. Counts and variable
definitions are in the [data dictionary](data_dictionary.md).

## Historical analysis

The analysis moved from workshop-specific overall PRE/POST change to item-level
learning maps, then to item-rest relationships and cohort comparability. These
steps asked whether total-score change was distributed across content and
whether the historical items related consistently to each workshop's total.

POST dimensional exploration used Spearman item relationships. It is
exploratory: it does not validate a single latent scale, and a descriptive
total should not be mistaken for proof of unidimensionality. Anxiety item
comparability across cohorts remains uncertain; Decision-making and Risk are
also distinct instruments, not comparable item scales. The
[conceptual framework](conceptual_framework.md) distinguishes immediate
knowledge from later recognition, application, and behaviour; the
[evidence-to-design synthesis](evidence_to_instrument_design.md) connects
Track A diagnostics to future measurement questions.

The repeated same-item, immediate post-test design means PRE/POST change is
observed change, not an isolated causal workshop effect. See
[limitations and future work](limitations_and_future_work.md).

## New instrument design

Develop three separate instruments, starting with Anxiety, then
Decision-making; Risk remains secondary. Begin with workshop objectives and a
test blueprint, then develop candidate items, conduct structured content
review and pilot the items.

Multiple choice is proposed to standardise scoring and support item-level
analysis; it measures recognition rather than free recall. This fits the aim
of introducing concepts participants may recognise later, but test performance
does not establish application in real situations. The proposed paper format
collects item-by-item Yes/No perceived-knowledge judgements before participants
see the options, rather than using a finer 1–4 confidence scale. Feasibility
and section order remain to be agreed. New item-level judgements could support
a perceived-by-correct response table; the historical aggregate count cannot.

Vignettes may later measure application; keep application and conceptual
knowledge separate unless empirical evidence supports combining them. The
[instrument development programme](../instrument/README.md) describes proposed
forms and their current status.
